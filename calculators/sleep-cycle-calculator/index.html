Sleep Cycle Calculator

The Sleep Cycle Calculator finds the best times to go to sleep or wake up, based on 90-minute sleep cycles, so you wake at the end of a cycle instead of in the middle of one.

What it is used for

It is used to plan bedtime for a chosen wake-up time, or to pick a good wake-up time for a chosen bedtime, aiming to feel less groggy in the morning.

Formula

Each sleep cycle lasts 90 minutes, and the calculator allows 14 minutes to fall asleep. For a wake time, bedtimes = wake time − (cycles × 90 + 14) minutes, for 4, 5, or 6 cycles. For a sleep time, wake times = sleep time + (cycles × 90 + 14) minutes, for 4, 5, or 6 cycles. Six cycles (about 9 hours) is marked ideal.

Worked example

Inputs: mode 'I want to wake at…', Wake-up Time 06:30. Step 1: for 6 cycles, go back 6 × 90 + 14 = 554 minutes from 06:30, which is 21:16 the previous evening. Step 2: for 5 cycles, go back 5 × 90 + 14 = 464 minutes, which is 22:46. Step 3: for 4 cycles, go back 4 × 90 + 14 = 374 minutes, which is 00:16. Final result: best bedtimes are 21:16 (ideal, 6 cycles), 22:46 (5 cycles), and 00:16 (4 cycles).

How to use

  1. Choose the mode: 'I want to wake at…' or 'I want to sleep at…' at the top of the tool.
  2. Enter your time in the Wake-up Time or sleep-time field — tap Now to fill in the current time.
  3. Tap Calculate to see the suggested times.
  4. Pick the slot with 6 cycles when you can; it is marked as the ideal choice.
  5. Go to bed at the suggested time, not just when you feel sleepy, so the cycles line up.

Tips

  • Six cycles is about 9 hours in bed, which suits most adults; five cycles is about 7 hours and 44 minutes.
  • Keep the same wake time every day, including weekends, to steady your body clock.
  • Avoid bright screens and heavy meals in the last hour before the suggested bedtime.
  • If you regularly need an alarm to wake up, you are probably cutting a cycle short.
  • This tool plans timing only. If you have ongoing sleep problems, speak to a doctor.

Common mistakes

  • Aiming for 8 hours exactly, which lands in the middle of a cycle and can leave you groggy.
  • Forgetting the 14 minutes to fall asleep and going to bed at the wake-up time minus exactly 7.5 hours.
  • Changing bedtime every night, so no regular rhythm builds up.
  • Using the calculator once and ignoring it — the benefit comes from using the same times daily.

Frequently asked questions

Why 90 minutes per cycle?

Sleep runs in cycles of roughly 90 minutes, moving through light sleep, deep sleep, and dream sleep. Waking at the end of a cycle, during light sleep, usually feels easier than waking from deep sleep.

Is 6 cycles really the best?

Six cycles is about 9 hours in bed and is marked ideal by the tool, but most adults do well on 5 cycles (about 7 hours 44 minutes). The right number varies from person to person.

What if I take longer than 14 minutes to fall asleep?

Then shift the bedtime earlier by the extra time you usually need. The 14 minutes is an average, not a rule for everyone.
