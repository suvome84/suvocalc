Salary Calculator

The Salary Calculator works out your monthly take-home pay from your gross annual salary after deducting provident fund (PF), income tax under the Budget 2025 new regime, and professional tax.

What it is used for

It is used to check how much of a salary offer actually reaches your bank account each month, to compare two job offers with different CTCs, and to plan monthly spending around the real take-home figure.

Monthly Take-Home

—

Formula

PF deduction = Gross Salary × PF%. Income tax is calculated on the new regime 2025-26 slabs (0% up to ₹4 lakh, 5% to ₹8 lakh, 10% to ₹12 lakh, 15% to ₹16 lakh, 20% to ₹20 lakh, 25% to ₹24 lakh, 30% above), with zero tax up to ₹12 lakh under Section 87A. Take-home = Gross − PF − Income Tax (plus 4% cess) − Professional Tax. Monthly take-home = Annual take-home ÷ 12.

Worked example

Inputs: Gross Annual Salary = ₹6,00,000, PF Contribution = 12%, Professional Tax = ₹2,400 per year. Step 1: PF = 6,00,000 × 12% = ₹72,000. Step 2: income tax = ₹0, because ₹6,00,000 is below the ₹12 lakh zero-tax limit under Section 87A. Step 3: annual take-home = 6,00,000 − 72,000 − 0 − 2,400 = ₹5,25,600. Step 4: monthly take-home = 5,25,600 ÷ 12 = ₹43,800.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter your total yearly salary in the "Gross Annual Salary (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter your PF contribution rate in the "PF Contribution (%)" field — 12% is the usual rate.
  3. Step 3: Enter your yearly professional tax in the "Professional Tax (₹/year)" field — ₹2,400 is common in many states.
  4. Step 4: Tap Calculate to see your monthly take-home pay.
  5. Step 5: Read the breakdown: annual CTC, PF deduction, income tax, professional tax, and annual take-home.

Tips

  • CTC and take-home pay are different — PF, tax, and professional tax are deducted before the money reaches you, so always budget from the take-home figure.
  • Under the Budget 2025 new regime there is zero income tax up to ₹12 lakh of income because of the Section 87A rebate.
  • Your PF deduction is not lost money — it is your own long-term saving, plus your employer contributes too.
  • Professional tax varies by state and is often ₹2,400 per year, but some states charge less or nothing.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the CTC as the monthly salary — CTC is annual, and the monthly take-home is much lower after deductions.
  • Forgetting that the tax shown uses the new regime 2025-26 slabs; the old regime gives a different result.
  • Entering the PF as a rupee amount instead of a percentage.
  • Ignoring professional tax and other small deductions when planning monthly expenses.

Frequently asked questions

What is the take-home salary on a ₹6,00,000 annual salary?

With 12% PF (₹72,000) and ₹2,400 professional tax, and zero income tax under the new regime up to ₹12 lakh, the annual take-home is ₹5,25,600, which is ₹43,800 per month.

Why is my take-home pay less than my CTC?

CTC includes your employer's contributions and benefits, while take-home pay is what remains after your PF contribution, income tax, and professional tax are deducted from your gross salary.

Is there really zero income tax up to ₹12 lakh?

Yes, under the Budget 2025 new regime for FY 2025-26, the Section 87A rebate makes the tax zero for total income up to ₹12 lakh. Income above that is taxed at the new regime slab rates.
