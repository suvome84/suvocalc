Simple Interest Calculator

The Simple Interest Calculator works out the interest earned or payable on a principal amount at a fixed annual rate over a number of years, where interest is charged only on the original principal.

What it is used for

It is used for short-term loans, fixed deposits that pay simple interest, and quick interest estimates where the interest does not itself earn interest.

Simple Interest

—

Formula

Simple Interest SI = P × R × T ÷ 100, where P is the principal, R is the annual rate in percent, and T is the time in years. Total Amount = P + SI.

Worked example

Inputs: Principal = ₹50,000, Rate = 7% per year, Time = 3 years. Step 1: SI = 50,000 × 7 × 3 ÷ 100. Step 2: 50,000 × 7 = 3,50,000. Step 3: 3,50,000 × 3 ÷ 100 = ₹10,500. Step 4: total amount = 50,000 + 10,500 = ₹60,500.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter the starting amount in the "Principal (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter the annual interest rate in the "Rate (% per year)" field.
  3. Step 3: Enter the duration in the "Time (Years)" field.
  4. Step 4: Tap Calculate to see the simple interest, the principal, and the total amount.

Tips

  • Simple interest is the same amount every year, so you can check the result by multiplying one year's interest by the number of years.
  • If the time is in months, convert it to years first (for example, 18 months is 1.5 years).
  • Use this calculator when interest is paid out or charged on the original amount only; use the compound interest calculator when interest is added to the balance.
  • For the same rate and period, simple interest is always less than compound interest.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the time in months instead of years — the formula needs years.
  • Forgetting to divide by 100 when using the rate as a percentage.
  • Using this calculator for a loan or deposit that actually compounds — the result will understate the real interest.
  • Mixing up the interest amount with the total amount: the total is principal plus interest, not the interest alone.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for simple interest?

Simple Interest = Principal × Rate × Time ÷ 100, where the rate is the annual percentage rate and the time is in years. The total amount to be repaid or received is the principal plus the simple interest.

How much interest is earned on ₹50,000 at 7% for 3 years?

The interest is 50,000 × 7 × 3 ÷ 100 = ₹10,500, and the total amount is ₹60,500.

When is simple interest used instead of compound interest?

Simple interest is used for short-term loans, some fixed deposits, and situations where interest is paid out regularly instead of being added to the balance. Most long-term savings and loans use compound interest.
