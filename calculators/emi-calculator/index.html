EMI / Loan Calculator

The EMI Calculator works out the fixed monthly payment (EMI) for a home loan, car loan, or personal loan, along with the total amount you will pay and the total interest.

What it is used for

It is used before taking a loan to check whether the monthly instalment fits your budget, and to compare how different loan amounts, interest rates, and tenures change what you pay overall.

Monthly EMI

—

Formula

EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the tenure in months. Total payment = EMI × n. Total interest = Total payment − P. At 0% interest, EMI = P ÷ n. Enter the tenure in months, or switch the Tenure Unit to Years.

Worked example

Inputs: Loan Amount = ₹5,00,000, Annual Interest Rate = 8.5%, Tenure = 60 months. Step 1: monthly rate r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083. Step 2: (1+r)^60 = 1.527. Step 3: EMI = 5,00,000 × 0.007083 × 1.527 ÷ (1.527 − 1) = ₹10,258.27 per month (about ₹10,258). Step 4: total payment = 10,258.27 × 60 = ₹6,15,496. Step 5: total interest = 6,15,496 − 5,00,000 = ₹1,15,496.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter the amount you want to borrow in the "Loan Amount (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter the annual interest rate in the "Annual Interest Rate (%)" field.
  3. Enter the repayment period and choose Months or Years for the Tenure Unit.
  4. Step 4: Tap Calculate to see your monthly EMI.
  5. Step 5: Read the breakdown below the result: total payment, total interest, principal, and interest as a percentage of the loan.

Tips

  • A longer tenure lowers the EMI but increases the total interest you pay — check the total interest, not just the EMI.
  • Even a 0.5% lower interest rate can save a large amount on a long home loan, so compare rates from more than one lender.
  • If you can afford a slightly higher EMI, a shorter tenure saves the most interest.
  • Keep your total EMIs within about half your monthly income so repayments stay comfortable.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to set the Tenure Unit — if you type 5, make sure the unit is Years, not Months.
  • Entering the monthly rate instead of the annual rate in the interest field.
  • Choosing a loan only by the lowest EMI and ignoring the much larger total interest over a long tenure.
  • Forgetting that the EMI does not include extra costs such as processing fees or insurance.

Frequently asked questions

What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan. Each EMI has two parts: interest on the outstanding loan and a part of the principal. Early EMIs are mostly interest; later EMIs repay more principal.

Does a longer tenure always mean a lower EMI?

Yes, spreading the loan over more months lowers the monthly EMI, but you pay interest for longer, so the total interest — and the total amount paid — goes up.

Can I reduce my EMI after taking a loan?

You can ask your lender to extend the tenure, refinance the loan at a lower rate, or make part-prepayments to reduce the principal, which lowers future interest. Each option has its own conditions, so confirm them with your lender.

Does the calculator work with 0% interest?

Yes. At 0% interest the EMI is simply the loan amount divided by the number of months — for example, ₹5,00,000 over 60 months is ₹8,333 per month.
