Loan Eligibility Calculator

The Loan Eligibility Calculator estimates the maximum loan amount a bank is likely to offer you, based on your monthly income, existing EMIs, interest rate, and tenure.

What it is used for

It is used before applying for a home loan, car loan, or personal loan to find a realistic borrowing limit, and to see how paying off an existing EMI or choosing a longer tenure changes that limit.

Max Loan Amount

—

Formula

Maximum EMI you can afford = (Monthly Income × 50%) − Existing Monthly EMIs. Maximum loan = Max EMI × ((1+r)^n − 1) ÷ (r × (1+r)^n), where r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the tenure in months. This uses the 50% FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio) rule most Indian banks apply.

Worked example

Inputs: Monthly Income = ₹50,000, Monthly EMI Obligations = ₹5,000, Annual Interest Rate = 8.5%, Loan Tenure = 20 years. Step 1: maximum EMI = 50,000 × 50% − 5,000 = ₹20,000. Step 2: monthly rate r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083, n = 240 months. Step 3: maximum loan = 20,000 × ((1.007083)^240 − 1) ÷ (0.007083 × (1.007083)^240) = ₹23,04,617.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter your monthly income in the "Monthly Income (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter all EMIs you already pay each month in the "Monthly EMI Obligations (₹)" field.
  3. Step 3: Enter the expected annual interest rate in the "Annual Interest Rate (%)" field.
  4. Step 4: Enter the repayment period in the "Loan Tenure (Years)" field.
  5. Step 5: Tap Calculate to see the maximum loan amount and the maximum EMI allowed.

Tips

  • Paying off or closing an existing loan raises your eligibility more than a small salary rise, because it frees up EMI capacity directly.
  • A longer tenure raises the eligible amount, but it also means paying much more interest over the life of the loan.
  • Banks also check your credit score, job stability, and the property value — this calculator shows the income-based limit only.
  • Borrowing the maximum you are eligible for is not always wise; leave room in your budget for emergencies.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out existing EMIs such as a bike loan or credit card EMI, which makes the eligible amount look higher than the bank will offer.
  • Entering gross salary instead of the monthly income the bank will count.
  • Entering the tenure in months instead of years — this calculator expects years.
  • Assuming the calculated amount is guaranteed — it is an estimate, and the bank's final sanction can be lower.

Frequently asked questions

How much loan can I get on a ₹50,000 monthly income?

With no other EMIs at 8.5% over 20 years, the 50% FOIR rule allows a maximum EMI of ₹25,000, which supports a loan of about ₹28.8 lakh. With ₹5,000 of existing EMIs, the limit falls to about ₹23.0 lakh.

What is the FOIR rule?

FOIR stands for Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio. Most Indian banks allow total fixed monthly obligations — all EMIs together — up to about 50% of your monthly income. This calculator uses that 50% limit.

Does my credit score affect loan eligibility?

Yes. This calculator only checks the income-based limit. Banks also look at your credit score, repayment history, employment type, and the value of the property or asset before sanctioning a loan.
