BMI Calculator

The BMI Calculator works out your Body Mass Index from your height and weight, and shows which weight category the result falls in.

What it is used for

It is used to get a quick, general sense of whether your weight is in a healthy range for your height, and to see the healthy weight range for your height.

BMI Score

—

Formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Categories used by the calculator: below 18.5 Underweight, 18.5–24.9 Normal weight, 25–29.9 Overweight, 30 and above Obese.

Worked example

Inputs: Height 170 cm, Weight 65 kg. Step 1: convert height to metres: 170 cm = 1.70 m. Step 2: square the height: 1.70 × 1.70 = 2.89. Step 3: divide weight by that number: 65 ÷ 2.89 = 22.49. Final result: BMI 22.49, which is Normal weight. Healthy weight range for 170 cm: 53.46–71.96 kg.

How to use

  1. Enter your height in the Height (cm) field, for example 170.
  2. Enter your weight in the Weight (kg) field, for example 65.
  3. Tap Calculate to see your BMI score and category.
  4. Read the healthy weight range shown under the result for your height.
  5. Compare your weight with that range instead of judging the BMI number alone.

Tips

  • Measure your height and weight in the morning, before eating, for the most consistent readings.
  • Use the same scale and the same units every time you re-check.
  • BMI is a screening number, not a diagnosis — a doctor looks at more than this one figure.
  • Muscular people can score high on BMI without carrying excess fat, because muscle weighs more than fat.
  • If your BMI is outside the normal range, talk to a doctor before starting any diet or exercise plan.

Common mistakes

  • Entering height in feet or inches instead of centimetres, which gives a wildly wrong BMI.
  • Typing weight in pounds instead of kilograms.
  • Treating the BMI category as a medical diagnosis instead of a rough guide.
  • Forgetting that BMI does not suit children, pregnant women, or very muscular athletes.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

The calculator treats 18.5 to 24.9 as Normal weight. Below 18.5 is Underweight, 25 to 29.9 is Overweight, and 30 or above is Obese.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI only uses height and weight, so it cannot tell fat from muscle. Athletes, bodybuilders, children, older adults, and pregnant women should not rely on it alone.

Does the calculator store my height and weight?

No. The calculation runs in your browser from the numbers you type, and nothing is sent anywhere.
