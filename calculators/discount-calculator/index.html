Discount Calculator

The Discount Calculator finds the final sale price after a percentage discount and shows exactly how much money you save.

What it is used for

It is used while shopping to check a sale price before buying, to compare two offers with different discount percentages, and to work out the real saving on a marked-down item.

Sale Price

—

Formula

Discount amount = Original Price × Discount ÷ 100. Sale Price = Original Price − Discount amount.

Worked example

Inputs: Original Price = ₹1,200, Discount = 25%. Step 1: discount amount = 1,200 × 25 ÷ 100 = ₹300. Step 2: sale price = 1,200 − 300 = ₹900. You save ₹300 and pay ₹900.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter the price before any discount in the "Original Price (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter the discount percentage in the "Discount (%)" field — for example, 25.
  3. Step 3: Tap Calculate to see the sale price.
  4. Step 4: Read the breakdown: how much you save, the original price, and the final price.

Tips

  • A 25% discount on a ₹1,200 item saves ₹300, but the same 25% on a ₹400 item saves only ₹100 — always check the rupee saving, not just the percentage.
  • Two successive discounts are not added together: 20% off then 10% off is 28% off in total, not 30%.
  • Compare the final price across shops rather than the discount percentage — a bigger percentage off a higher marked price can still cost more.
  • Check whether the discount applies before or after tax, because that changes the final amount you pay.

Common mistakes

  • Adding two discounts together (20% + 10% = 30%) instead of applying them one after the other.
  • Assuming a higher discount percentage always means a lower price — the marked price may be inflated.
  • Entering the discount as a rupee amount instead of a percentage.
  • Forgetting that the discount percentage must be between 0 and 100.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a 25% discount on ₹1,200?

Multiply 1,200 by 25 and divide by 100 to get the saving of ₹300, then subtract it from 1,200. The sale price is ₹900.

What is the sale price if something costs ₹2,000 with a 40% discount?

The discount is 2,000 × 40 ÷ 100 = ₹800, so the sale price is 2,000 − 800 = ₹1,200.

How do I find the discount percentage from the original and sale price?

Subtract the sale price from the original price, divide by the original price, and multiply by 100. For example, (1,200 − 900) ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 25%.
