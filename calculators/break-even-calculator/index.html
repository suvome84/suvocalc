Break-Even Calculator

The Break-Even Calculator finds how many units a business must sell each month to cover all its costs — the point where it stops losing money and starts making a profit.

What it is used for

It is used by small business owners and shopkeepers to set a sales target, to test whether a price covers costs, and to see the monthly profit or loss at the current level of sales.

Break-Even Point

—

Formula

Contribution margin per unit = Selling Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit. Break-even units = Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution margin per unit (rounded up). Break-even revenue = Break-even units × Selling Price. Contribution margin % = Contribution margin ÷ Selling Price × 100. Current monthly profit = (Current Units × Contribution margin) − Fixed Costs.

Worked example

Inputs: Fixed Costs = ₹50,000 per month, Selling Price per Unit = ₹500, Variable Cost per Unit = ₹200, Current Units Sold = 150 per month. Step 1: contribution margin = 500 − 200 = ₹300 per unit, which is 60% of the price. Step 2: break-even units = 50,000 ÷ 300 = 166.7, rounded up to 167 units. Step 3: break-even revenue = 167 × 500 = ₹83,500 per month. Step 4: current profit = 150 × 300 − 50,000 = −₹5,000, so the business is below break-even by 17 units.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter your monthly fixed costs — rent, salaries, electricity — in the "Fixed Costs (₹/month)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter what you charge for one item in the "Selling Price per Unit (₹)" field.
  3. Step 3: Enter what one item costs you to make or buy in the "Variable Cost per Unit (₹)" field.
  4. Step 4: Enter how many units you sell now in the "Current Units Sold/Month" field.
  5. Step 5: Tap Calculate to see the break-even point in units, the break-even revenue, and your current monthly profit.

Tips

  • Fixed costs stay the same whether you sell 10 units or 1,000, while variable costs grow with every unit — keep the two separate.
  • Raising the price or lowering the variable cost both raise the contribution margin and bring the break-even point down.
  • The break-even point is a monthly target: selling 167 units in a month means the month breaks even, not that each day does.
  • If you are below break-even, check first whether the price, the volume, or the fixed costs are the problem — each has a different fix.

Common mistakes

  • Putting variable costs such as raw material into the fixed costs field, which distorts the break-even point.
  • Setting the selling price at or below the variable cost — then every sale loses money and there is no break-even point.
  • Forgetting fixed costs such as rent or salaries when adding up monthly costs.
  • Treating the break-even point as a profit target — it is only the point where profit is zero.

Frequently asked questions

What is the break-even point?

It is the number of units you must sell for total revenue to exactly equal total costs. Below it you make a loss; above it you make a profit. For example, with ₹50,000 of fixed costs and a ₹300 contribution per unit, the break-even point is 167 units per month.

How do I calculate the break-even point?

Subtract the variable cost per unit from the selling price to get the contribution margin, then divide your fixed costs by that margin. Fixed costs of ₹50,000 divided by a ₹300 margin gives 167 units.

What is contribution margin?

It is the amount each unit sold contributes toward covering fixed costs and then profit: selling price minus variable cost per unit. A ₹500 item costing ₹200 to make has a contribution margin of ₹300, or 60%.
