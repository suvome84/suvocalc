SIP Calculator

The SIP Calculator projects how much a Systematic Investment Plan in a mutual fund can grow when you invest a fixed amount every month at an expected annual return.

What it is used for

It is used to plan monthly investing, to see the maturity value of a SIP over several years, and to compare how a higher monthly amount, a higher return, or a longer period changes the final amount.

Maturity Value

—

Formula

Maturity Value FV = P × ((1+i)^n − 1) ÷ i × (1+i), where P is the monthly investment, i is the monthly return (expected annual return ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the number of months. Total invested = P × n. Wealth gained = FV − Total invested.

Worked example

Inputs: Monthly Investment = ₹5,000, Expected Annual Return = 12%, Investment Period = 10 years. Step 1: monthly return i = 12 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.01, n = 120 months. Step 2: FV = 5,000 × ((1.01)^120 − 1) ÷ 0.01 × 1.01. Step 3: (1.01)^120 = 3.3004, so FV = 5,000 × 230.04 × 1.01 = ₹11,61,695. Step 4: total invested = 5,000 × 120 = ₹6,00,000. Step 5: wealth gained = 11,61,695 − 6,00,000 = ₹5,61,695.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter the amount you will invest each month in the "Monthly Investment (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter the return you expect per year in the "Expected Annual Return (%)" field.
  3. Step 3: Enter how many years you will keep investing in the "Investment Period (Years)" field.
  4. Step 4: Tap Calculate to see the maturity value.
  5. Step 5: Read the breakdown: total invested, wealth gained, growth percentage, and the monthly amount with the number of months.

Tips

  • Starting early matters more than investing a large amount late — the extra years of compounding do most of the work.
  • The expected return is an assumption, not a promise — mutual fund returns vary, so try 10% and 12% to see a range of outcomes.
  • Increasing your monthly SIP a little every year (a step-up SIP) can raise the final amount a lot over a long period.
  • Staying invested through market falls matters more than timing the market — SIPs work by buying through ups and downs.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the projected maturity value as guaranteed — it depends on the return you entered, and real returns change every year.
  • Stopping the SIP during a market fall, which removes the benefit of buying units at lower prices.
  • Entering the annual return as a monthly return, which makes the projection far too high.
  • Comparing SIPs only by the final amount without checking the total invested — the gain is the final amount minus what you put in.

Frequently asked questions

How much will ₹5,000 per month grow to in 10 years at 12%?

At a steady 12% annual return, a ₹5,000 monthly SIP grows to about ₹11,61,695 in 10 years. You invest ₹6,00,000 and the gain is about ₹5,61,695. Real returns vary, so this is a projection, not a guarantee.

What is a SIP?

A SIP, or Systematic Investment Plan, is a way to invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month. It spreads your investment over time, so you buy units at different prices, and your money benefits from compounding.

Is the return on a SIP guaranteed?

No. SIPs invest in market-linked mutual funds, so returns go up and down with the market. The calculator shows what would happen at the return rate you enter — use it as a plan, not a promise.
