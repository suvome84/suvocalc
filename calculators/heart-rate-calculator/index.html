Heart Rate / Max HR Zone

The Heart Rate Calculator works out your maximum heart rate from your age and splits it into training zones, using the Karvonen method with your resting heart rate.

What it is used for

It is used to plan exercise intensity: training in the right zone for recovery, fat burning, cardio fitness, or peak effort, instead of guessing how hard to push.

Max Heart Rate (220-age)

—

Formula

Maximum heart rate = 220 − age. Heart rate reserve = maximum heart rate − resting heart rate. Zone 1 (Recovery): resting + 50–60% of reserve. Zone 2 (Fat Burn): resting + 60–70%. Zone 3 (Cardio): resting + 70–80%. Zone 4 (Peak): resting + 80–90%.

Worked example

Inputs: Age 25 years, Resting Heart Rate 65 bpm. Step 1: maximum heart rate = 220 − 25 = 195 bpm. Step 2: heart rate reserve = 195 − 65 = 130 bpm. Step 3: Zone 2 (Fat Burn) = 65 + 60% of 130 to 65 + 70% of 130 = 143 to 156 bpm. Final result: max heart rate 195 bpm, with zones 130–143 (Recovery), 143–156 (Fat Burn), 156–169 (Cardio), and 169–182 (Peak). This is a general fitness guide, not medical advice.

How to use

  1. Enter your age in the Age (years) field, for example 25.
  2. Enter your resting heart rate in the Resting Heart Rate (bpm) field — measure it in the morning before getting up.
  3. Tap Calculate to see your maximum heart rate.
  4. Read the four training zones shown under the result.
  5. Use a watch or fitness band during exercise to stay inside your chosen zone.

Tips

  • Measure resting heart rate first thing in the morning, lying still, for three days and average the readings.
  • Beginners should spend most training time in Zones 1 and 2 before adding Zone 3 and 4 work.
  • Warm up for 5 to 10 minutes before reaching your target zone.
  • Stop and rest if you feel dizzy, chest pain, or unusual breathlessness — and seek medical help if it continues.
  • If you have a heart condition or take heart medicine, ask your doctor which zones are safe for you.

Common mistakes

  • Using 220 minus age as an exact personal maximum — it is a population average and can be off by 10 or more beats.
  • Skipping the resting heart rate and using rough zones that do not fit your fitness level.
  • Training in Zone 4 every session, which leads to exhaustion instead of fitness.
  • Measuring resting heart rate after coffee or a walk, which inflates it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a normal resting heart rate?

For most adults it is roughly 60 to 100 beats per minute. Trained athletes are often lower, around 40 to 60. A single reading outside this range is not a diagnosis — check with a doctor if you are concerned.

Which zone burns the most fat?

Zone 2 is labelled Fat Burn because a higher share of the energy comes from fat at that intensity. But total calories burned also matter, and higher zones burn more calories overall.

Is 220 minus age accurate?

It is a widely used estimate, not an exact figure for any one person. Your true maximum can differ by 10 to 15 beats. Treat the zones as a guide.
