Income Tax Calculator (India)

The Income Tax Calculator works out the income tax payable in India for FY 2025-26, comparing the new regime (Budget 2025), the new regime for 2024-25, and the old regime, including the 4% health and education cess.

What it is used for

It is used to estimate the annual tax on a salary or income, to see the monthly tax deduction, and to compare which regime — new or old — gives the lower tax for a given income.

—

Formula

New Regime 2025-26 slabs: 0% up to ₹4 lakh, 5% on ₹4–8 lakh, 10% on ₹8–12 lakh, 15% on ₹12–16 lakh, 20% on ₹16–20 lakh, 25% on ₹20–24 lakh, 30% above ₹24 lakh. Standard deduction: ₹75,000 for salaried/pensioner income under the new regime (₹50,000 under the old regime), so salaried taxpayers pay zero tax up to ₹12.75 lakh gross under the new regime. Section 87A rebate makes the tax zero for taxable income up to ₹12 lakh (new regime) or ₹5 lakh (old regime), with marginal relief just above those limits. Surcharge: 10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore (37% above ₹5 crore under the old regime only). Total tax = tax + surcharge + 4% cess. Old regime slabs: 0% up to ₹2.5 lakh (₹3 lakh for 60–80 years, ₹5 lakh above 80), 5% to ₹5 lakh, 20% to ₹10 lakh, 30% above ₹10 lakh; HRA and Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh) reduce old-regime taxable income.

Worked example

Inputs: Annual Income = ₹15,00,000 (salaried), Tax Regime = New Regime 2025-26. Step 1: standard deduction ₹75,000, so taxable income = 15,00,000 − 75,000 = ₹14,25,000. Step 2: tax on ₹4–8 lakh at 5% = ₹20,000. Step 3: tax on ₹8–12 lakh at 10% = ₹40,000. Step 4: tax on ₹12–14.25 lakh at 15% = ₹33,750. Step 5: tax before cess = 20,000 + 40,000 + 33,750 = ₹93,750. Step 6: cess at 4% = ₹3,750. Step 7: total annual tax = ₹97,500, which is ₹8,125 per month.

How to use

  1. Enter your total annual income in the Annual Income (₹) field.
  2. Choose the Tax Regime — New Regime 2025-26, New Regime 2024-25, or Old Regime.
  3. Choose your Employment Type: Salaried / Pensioner gets the standard deduction; Other does not.
  4. Choose your Age Group — it changes the basic exemption limit under the old regime.
  5. For the old regime only, enter any HRA exemption and Section 80C deductions you claim.
  6. Tap Calculate to see your annual tax, with the taxable income, surcharge and cess shown below the result.

Tips

  • Under Budget 2025, the new regime charges zero tax up to ₹12 lakh of income because of the Section 87A rebate.
  • The old regime can still be better if you claim large deductions such as HRA, Section 80C, and home loan interest — compare both before choosing.
  • The 4% health and education cess is charged on the tax amount, not on your income.
  • Surcharge is included for incomes above ₹50 lakh; special income such as capital gains is not covered — it is taxed at separate rates.

Common mistakes

  • Applying the top slab rate to the whole income instead of only the part that falls in that slab.
  • Forgetting the 4% cess, which adds to the tax.
  • Choosing a regime without comparing — the new regime has lower rates but almost no deductions, while the old regime keeps the deductions.
  • Entering monthly income in the annual income field, which makes the tax look far too low.

Frequently asked questions

Is there really zero income tax up to ₹12 lakh?

Yes. Under the Budget 2025 new regime for FY 2025-26, the Section 87A rebate brings the tax to zero for taxable income up to ₹12 lakh. For salaried people the ₹75,000 standard deduction is taken first, so gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh is tax-free. Above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief caps the tax at the amount by which income exceeds ₹12 lakh.

What are the new regime tax slabs for 2025-26?

0% up to ₹4 lakh, 5% on ₹4–8 lakh, 10% on ₹8–12 lakh, 15% on ₹12–16 lakh, 20% on ₹16–20 lakh, 25% on ₹20–24 lakh, and 30% above ₹24 lakh, plus a 4% health and education cess on the tax.

Which regime is better, new or old?

It depends on your deductions. The new regime has lower slab rates and zero tax up to ₹12 lakh, but it allows almost no deductions. The old regime keeps deductions such as HRA, Section 80C, and home loan interest, so it can win if your deductions are large. Run both in this calculator and compare.

What is the 4% cess?

It is the health and education cess charged at 4% of the income tax amount. For example, on a tax of ₹1,05,000 the cess is ₹4,200, making the total ₹1,09,200.

What is marginal relief?

If your taxable income is just above ₹12 lakh, the tax cannot exceed the amount above ₹12 lakh. For example, at ₹12,05,000 taxable income the slab tax would be ₹60,750, but marginal relief caps it at ₹5,000 (plus 4% cess).

Does the old regime have a standard deduction?

Yes — ₹50,000 for salaried and pensioner income. The old regime also lets you reduce taxable income with HRA exemption and Section 80C deductions up to ₹1.5 lakh, and it has higher basic exemption limits for senior citizens (₹3 lakh for 60–80 years, ₹5 lakh above 80).
