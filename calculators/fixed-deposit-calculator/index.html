Fixed Deposit Calculator

The Fixed Deposit Calculator works out the maturity amount, interest earned, and effective annual yield of a bank fixed deposit, with simple interest or compounding monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, or annually.

What it is used for

It is used to compare fixed deposit offers from different banks, to see how the compounding frequency changes the return, and to plan the maturity value of a lump-sum deposit.

Formula

With compounding: Maturity = P × (1 + R ÷ (n × 100))^(n×T), where P is the principal, R is the annual rate, n is the compounding frequency per year (12 monthly, 4 quarterly, 2 half-yearly, 1 annually), and T is the tenure in years. With simple interest: Interest = P × R × T ÷ 100 and Maturity = P + Interest. Effective yield = (Maturity ÷ P)^(1 ÷ T) − 1, as a percentage.

Worked example

Inputs: Principal Amount = ₹1,00,000, Interest Rate = 7.5% per year, Tenure = 3 years, Compounding = Quarterly. Step 1: maturity = 1,00,000 × (1 + 7.5 ÷ 400)^12. Step 2: (1.01875)^12 = 1.2497. Step 3: maturity = ₹1,24,972. Step 4: interest earned = 1,24,972 − 1,00,000 = ₹24,972. Step 5: effective yield = (1,24,972 ÷ 1,00,000)^(1 ÷ 3) − 1 = 7.71% per year. FD rates vary by bank and change over time — check the current rate.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter the deposit amount in the "Principal Amount (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter the annual interest rate in the "Interest Rate (% p.a.)" field.
  3. Step 3: Enter the deposit period in the "Tenure (Years)" field.
  4. Step 4: Choose the compounding frequency from the "Compounding" dropdown — Quarterly, Monthly, Half-Yearly, Annually, or Simple Interest.
  5. Step 5: Tap Calculate to see the maturity amount, the interest earned, and the effective yield.

Tips

  • Quarterly compounding is the most common for bank FDs in India; monthly compounding gives a slightly higher maturity at the same rate.
  • The effective yield is higher than the headline rate when interest compounds — compare effective yields, not just headline rates.
  • Senior citizens usually get a higher FD rate, often 0.50% more — check your bank's senior citizen rates if they apply.
  • Breaking an FD early usually costs a penalty of around 0.5% to 1% on the rate, so choose a tenure you can hold.

Common mistakes

  • Using an old FD rate — rates change with the bank and over time, so always check the current rate before calculating.
  • Choosing "Simple Interest" when the bank actually compounds quarterly, which understates the maturity amount.
  • Forgetting that the interest is taxable income in the year it is earned, even if it is only paid at maturity.
  • Comparing FDs only by the headline rate without checking the compounding frequency.

Frequently asked questions

How much will ₹1,00,000 grow to in 3 years at 7.5% compounded quarterly?

It grows to about ₹1,24,972, earning ₹24,972 in interest. The effective yield is about 7.71% per year.

What is the difference between the FD rate and the effective yield?

The FD rate is the nominal annual rate, while the effective yield is the actual yearly return after compounding. With quarterly compounding at 7.5%, the effective yield is 7.71%.

Is FD interest taxable?

Yes. Fixed deposit interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. Banks also deduct TDS if the interest in a year crosses the threshold.
