Crypto Calculator

The Crypto Calculator works out how many coins an investment buys, what the holding is worth at a current or target price, and the profit or loss on the investment.

What it is used for

It is used to check the value of a crypto holding, to see the gain or loss at a target price before selling, and to find the break-even price at which the investment neither gains nor loses.

Coins Purchased

—

Formula

Coins purchased = Investment Amount ÷ Buy Price per coin. Current value = Coins × Current or Target Price. Profit or Loss = Current value − Investment Amount. Return % = Profit ÷ Investment × 100. Break-even price = Buy Price per coin.

Worked example

Inputs: Cryptocurrency = Bitcoin (BTC), Investment Amount = ₹10,000, Buy Price = ₹50,00,000 per coin, Current/Target Price = ₹60,00,000 per coin. Step 1: coins = 10,000 ÷ 50,00,000 = 0.002 BTC. Step 2: current value = 0.002 × 60,00,000 = ₹12,000. Step 3: profit = 12,000 − 10,000 = ₹2,000, which is a 20% return. Step 4: break-even price = ₹50,00,000 per coin.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Choose the cryptocurrency from the "Cryptocurrency" dropdown — Bitcoin (BTC), Ethereum (ETH), BNB, Solana (SOL), or Other.
  2. Step 2: Enter the amount you invested in the "Investment Amount (₹)" field.
  3. Step 3: Enter the price you bought at in the "Buy Price (₹ per coin)" field.
  4. Step 4: Enter the current price, or the price you are targeting, in the "Current/Target Price (₹)" field.
  5. Step 5: Tap Calculate to see the coins purchased, the current value, and the profit or loss.

Tips

  • Crypto prices move fast — a result based on a price from an hour ago may already be out of date, so use the latest price.
  • The break-even price is simply your buy price, so any price above it is a gain and any price below it is a loss.
  • This calculator ignores exchange fees and taxes, which reduce the real profit.
  • Never invest money you cannot afford to lose — crypto is far more volatile than a fixed deposit or a savings account.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the price of one coin in the wrong currency or unit, which makes the coin count wildly wrong.
  • Using an old price as the current price and believing the profit shown.
  • Forgetting that buying and selling on an exchange involves fees that eat into the profit.
  • Confusing the total investment with the price per coin — the two fields are different.

Frequently asked questions

How many Bitcoins do I get for ₹10,000 if Bitcoin costs ₹50,00,000?

You get 10,000 ÷ 50,00,000 = 0.002 BTC. If the price rises to ₹60,00,000, that holding is worth ₹12,000, a profit of ₹2,000 or 20%.

What is the break-even price in crypto?

It is the price at which your holding is worth exactly what you invested — the same as your buy price per coin. Above it you are in profit; below it you are in loss.

Does this calculator include exchange fees?

No. It calculates from the investment amount and the buy and sell prices only. Exchange fees and taxes are not included, so your real profit will be slightly lower.
