Budget Planner (50/30/20)

The Budget Planner splits your monthly income using the 50/30/20 rule — 50% for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings — and compares that plan with what you actually spend and save today.

What it is used for

It is used to set a realistic monthly budget, to see whether your current saving rate is healthy, and to find how much money is left over after expenses and savings.

Recommended Monthly Savings

—

Formula

Needs = Monthly Income × 50%. Wants = Monthly Income × 30%. Savings = Monthly Income × 20%. Your savings rate = (Current Monthly Savings ÷ Monthly Income) × 100. Monthly surplus = Income − Current Expenses − Current Savings.

Worked example

Inputs: Monthly Income = ₹50,000, Current Monthly Expenses = ₹35,000, Current Monthly Savings = ₹5,000. Step 1: needs = 50,000 × 50% = ₹25,000. Step 2: wants = 50,000 × 30% = ₹15,000. Step 3: recommended savings = 50,000 × 20% = ₹10,000. Step 4: your savings rate = 5,000 ÷ 50,000 × 100 = 10%, rated Good. Step 5: monthly surplus = 50,000 − 35,000 − 5,000 = ₹10,000.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter your total monthly income in the "Monthly Income (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter what you currently spend each month in the "Current Monthly Expenses (₹)" field.
  3. Step 3: Enter what you currently save each month in the "Current Monthly Savings (₹)" field.
  4. Step 4: Tap Calculate to see your 50/30/20 split.
  5. Step 5: Read your savings rate grade and your monthly surplus to see where you stand.

Tips

  • If your needs take more than 50% of your income, focus on the biggest need — usually rent or food — rather than cutting small wants first.
  • Treat savings as a fixed bill: move the 20% to a separate account on payday instead of saving whatever is left at month-end.
  • A savings rate of 20% or more is rated Excellent by this calculator; 10% or more is Good, and below 5% needs improvement.
  • Review the budget every month for the first three months — the first plan is a guess, and the real numbers will correct it.

Common mistakes

  • Putting wants such as eating out or streaming subscriptions into the needs bucket, which makes the budget look balanced when it is not.
  • Setting a savings target you cannot keep for even one month — a smaller target kept every month beats a big one abandoned in week two.
  • Forgetting irregular yearly costs such as insurance premiums or festival spending when adding up monthly expenses.
  • Using gross salary instead of take-home pay as the monthly income, which overstates what you can spend.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?

It is a simple way to divide your take-home pay: 50% for needs such as rent, food, and transport, 30% for wants such as eating out and entertainment, and 20% for savings and repaying debt. It is a guideline, not a strict law — adjust the shares to your situation.

What is a good savings rate?

Saving 20% of your income is considered excellent, 10% is good, and 5% is fair. Anything below 5% leaves little room for emergencies, so the first goal is usually to reach 10%.

My needs are more than 50% of my income. What should I do?

The 50/30/20 split is a target, not a rule you must hit at once. Start by saving a small fixed amount each month, then work on the largest need — often rent or transport — and move toward the split gradually.
