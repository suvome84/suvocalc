Unit Price Comparator

The Unit Price Comparator compares two products by their price per unit — per gram, per millilitre, or per piece — to show which one is really cheaper.

What it is used for

It is used while grocery shopping to decide between two pack sizes or brands, by comparing what each one costs per gram, per millilitre, or per piece.

Formula

Unit Price = Price ÷ Quantity. Compare the two unit prices: the lower one is the better deal. Saving per unit = |unit price of A − unit price of B|.

Worked example

Inputs: Product A — Price ₹120, Quantity 500 g; Product B — Price ₹200, Quantity 1000 g. Step 1: unit price of A = 120 ÷ 500 = ₹0.24 per gram. Step 2: unit price of B = 200 ÷ 1000 = ₹0.20 per gram. Step 3: B is cheaper by 0.24 − 0.20 = ₹0.04 per gram. Final result: Product B is the better deal, saving ₹0.04 per gram.

How to use

  1. Enter Product A's price in the Product A — Price (₹) field, for example 120.
  2. Enter Product A's quantity in the Product A — Quantity field, for example 500, and its unit (g, ml, or pcs).
  3. Do the same for Product B — Price (₹), Quantity, and Unit.
  4. Tap Calculate to see both unit prices.
  5. Read which product wins and how much you save per unit.

Tips

  • In a supermarket, check the small per-unit price printed on the shelf label — it is the same comparison.
  • Convert both quantities to the same unit before entering them.
  • The bigger pack is usually cheaper per unit, but not always — check every time.
  • Use pieces (pcs) as the unit for tablets, diapers, and other counted items.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing the packet prices instead of the per-unit prices.
  • Mixing units, such as grams for one product and kilograms for the other.
  • Forgetting that the bigger pack can be worse value when its unit price is higher.
  • Ignoring quality and expiry date — the cheapest per gram is not always the best buy.

Frequently asked questions

Which product is the better deal?

The cheaper unit price, not the cheaper packet. A big pack can cost more overall while still being cheaper per gram — that is exactly what this tool checks.

What if one product is in kg and the other in grams?

The units must match: compare grams with grams, or millilitres with millilitres. If one is in kg and the other in grams, convert first.

Can I compare items sold by count, like tablets?

Yes — use pieces as the quantity, for example 10 tablets for ₹50 versus 20 tablets for ₹90.
