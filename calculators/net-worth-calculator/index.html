Net Worth Calculator

The Net Worth Calculator adds up everything you own — cash, investments, property, and other assets — and subtracts everything you owe — home loans, personal and car loans, and credit card dues — to give your net worth.

What it is used for

It is used to take a snapshot of your financial position, to track whether your wealth is growing over time, and to see how much of your assets is funded by debt through the debt-to-asset ratio.

Formula

Total Assets = Cash & Savings + Investments & Stocks + Property Value + Other Assets. Total Liabilities = Home Loan Outstanding + Personal / Car Loans + Credit Card Dues. Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities. Debt-to-Asset Ratio = Total Liabilities ÷ Total Assets × 100.

Worked example

Inputs: Cash & Savings = ₹2,00,000, Investments & Stocks = ₹5,00,000, Property Value = ₹30,00,000, Other Assets = ₹50,000, Home Loan Outstanding = ₹20,00,000, Personal / Car Loans = ₹3,00,000, Credit Card Dues = ₹50,000. Step 1: total assets = 2,00,000 + 5,00,000 + 30,00,000 + 50,000 = ₹37,50,000. Step 2: total liabilities = 20,00,000 + 3,00,000 + 50,000 = ₹23,50,000. Step 3: net worth = 37,50,000 − 23,50,000 = ₹14,00,000. Step 4: debt-to-asset ratio = 23,50,000 ÷ 37,50,000 × 100 = 62.7%.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter your bank balance and cash in the "Cash & Savings (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter shares, mutual funds, and other investments in the "Investments & Stocks (₹)" field.
  3. Step 3: Enter the current market value of your property in the "Property Value (₹)" field.
  4. Step 4: Enter any other assets in the "Other Assets (₹)" field.
  5. Step 5: Enter what you still owe in "Home Loan Outstanding (₹)", "Personal / Car Loans (₹)", and "Credit Card Dues (₹)", then tap Calculate to see your net worth.

Tips

  • Recalculate your net worth once or twice a year — the trend matters more than any single number.
  • Value property at a realistic market price, not at the price you paid or the price you hope for.
  • A negative net worth is a starting point, not a verdict — paying down the most expensive debt first moves it up fastest.
  • A debt-to-asset ratio above 50% means more than half your assets are funded by debt, which is worth watching closely.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out a loan or credit card balance, which makes the net worth look higher than it is.
  • Counting the full property value but forgetting the home loan owed on it.
  • Using the purchase price of an asset instead of its current value.
  • Counting money you expect to receive, such as a future salary or bonus, as an asset you already own.

Frequently asked questions

What is net worth?

Net worth is what you own minus what you owe. Add up all your assets — cash, investments, property, and other assets — and subtract all your liabilities — loans and credit card dues. The result is your net worth.

What is a good debt-to-asset ratio?

Lower is better. A ratio below 50% means you own more than half of your assets outright. Above 50% means most of your assets are funded by debt, which leaves less room for setbacks.

Can my net worth be negative?

Yes. If what you owe is more than what you own, your net worth is negative. It is common early in life, for example with a large home loan, and it improves as you repay debt and build assets.
