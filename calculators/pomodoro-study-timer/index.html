Pomodoro Study Timer

The Pomodoro Study Timer runs the classic Pomodoro technique: 25 minutes of focused study, then a 5-minute break, with a longer break after four sessions.

What it is used for

It is used by students to study in focused 25-minute blocks with regular breaks, so long study sessions stay productive instead of drifting.

Formula

Method, not a formula: one Pomodoro = 25 minutes of focused work + 5 minutes of short break. After four Pomodoros, take a 15-minute long break. The tool shows a progress ring, the time remaining, and a count of completed sessions.

Worked example

Use case: you have a 2-hour study session for an exam. Step 1: choose Pomodoro mode and tap Start — the timer runs for 25 minutes of focus. Step 2: when it ends, switch to Short Break for 5 minutes. Step 3: repeat the cycle — after four focus sessions you have done 4 × 25 = 100 minutes of focused study with 15 minutes of short breaks. Step 4: after the fourth session, take the Long Break of 15 minutes. Final result: 2 hours used, 100 minutes of real focus, and the sessions counter shows 4 completed sessions.

How to use

  1. Tap the Pomodoro button to choose the 25-minute focus mode, or pick Short Break (5 min) or Long Break (15 min).
  2. Tap Start to begin the countdown shown in the ring.
  3. Work on one task only until the timer ends — no phone, no switching tasks.
  4. When the focus time ends, take the short break before starting the next Pomodoro.
  5. Watch the Sessions completed counter to track how many focus blocks you finished.

Tips

  • Pick one clear task before starting the timer, for example 'finish 20 maths problems'.
  • Put your phone on silent during the 25 minutes so nothing interrupts the session.
  • Use the 5-minute break to stretch, drink water, or look away from the screen.
  • After four Pomodoros, take the full 15-minute long break — it keeps the next round productive.
  • If 25 minutes feels too long at first, start with two sessions a day and build up.

Common mistakes

  • Working through the break — the break is part of the method, not wasted time.
  • Starting a new Pomodoro while still thinking about the last task instead of resetting focus.
  • Choosing tasks that need more than 25 minutes and getting frustrated when the timer ends mid-task — split big tasks first.
  • Using the timer with the phone's notifications on, which breaks focus.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Pomodoro technique?

The classic Pomodoro method is 25 minutes of focused work followed by a 5-minute break, with a longer 15-minute break after four sessions. This tool uses exactly that pattern.

How do I stop or reset the timer?

Tap Reset to stop the current timer and return it to the start of the selected mode.

Can I use it just as a break timer?

Yes. Tap Short Break for a 5-minute break or Long Break for a 15-minute break, then tap Start.
