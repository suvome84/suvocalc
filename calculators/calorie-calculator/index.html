Calorie / TDEE Calculator

The Calorie Calculator estimates your daily calorie needs (TDEE) from your age, weight, height, gender, and activity level, using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

What it is used for

It is used to plan how many calories to eat each day to maintain weight, lose about 0.5 kg a week, or gain about 0.5 kg a week, and to see a daily protein target.

Daily Calories (TDEE)

—

Formula

BMR (men) = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5. BMR (women) = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161. TDEE = BMR × activity multiplier (Sedentary 1.2, Light 1.375, Moderate 1.55, Active 1.725, Very Active 1.9). Lose 0.5 kg/week: TDEE − 500. Gain 0.5 kg/week: TDEE + 500.

Worked example

Inputs: Age 25, Weight 70 kg, Height 170 cm, Gender Male, Activity Moderate (3–5 days/week). Step 1: BMR = 10 × 70 + 6.25 × 170 − 5 × 25 + 5 = 700 + 1062.5 − 125 + 5 = 1642.5 kcal. Step 2: TDEE = 1642.5 × 1.55 = 2545.9, rounded to 2546 kcal. Step 3: to lose 0.5 kg a week, 2546 − 500 = 2046 kcal a day. Final result: daily need about 2,546 kcal, with a protein target of about 126 g a day (70 × 1.8). These are estimates, not medical advice.

How to use

  1. Enter your age in the Age field, for example 25.
  2. Enter your weight in the Weight (kg) field and your height in the Height (cm) field.
  3. Choose your gender from the Gender dropdown.
  4. Pick the Activity Level that best matches your week, from Sedentary to Very Active.
  5. Tap Calculate and read your BMR, TDEE, and the lose/gain calorie figures.

Tips

  • Be honest about activity level — most desk workers are Sedentary or Light, not Moderate.
  • Use the TDEE as a starting point and adjust after two or three weeks of watching your actual weight change.
  • A 500 kcal daily deficit is the calculator's rule for about 0.5 kg a week; faster loss is harder to sustain.
  • Count calories for a week or two to learn portion sizes, then you can estimate by eye.
  • If you have a medical condition, ask a doctor or dietitian before changing your diet.

Common mistakes

  • Picking a higher activity level than you really have, which overstates your calorie needs.
  • Treating TDEE as exact — it is an estimate, and real needs vary by several hundred calories.
  • Cutting far below the lose-weight figure, which leaves you tired and hungry.
  • Forgetting that drinks, cooking oil, and snacks count toward the daily total.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is the calories your body burns at complete rest. TDEE is BMR multiplied by your activity level, so it is the calories you burn on a normal day.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

The calculator suggests TDEE minus 500 kcal a day, which aims for about 0.5 kg a week. This is a general estimate, not medical advice.

Why does gender change the result?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation uses different constants for men and women because average body composition differs. The tool applies +5 for men and −161 for women.
