Savings Goal Planner

The Savings Goal Planner works out how to reach a savings target: either how long it will take at a monthly saving amount you choose, or how much you must save each month to reach the goal by a date you choose.

What it is used for

It is used to plan for a specific goal such as a bike, a phone, a course fee, or a house deposit, and to see how interest on the savings shortens the time or lowers the monthly amount needed.

Formula

Fill in either Monthly Savings or Target Months, not both. With a monthly amount, the balance grows month by month as Balance = Balance × (1 + monthly rate) + Monthly Savings, until it reaches the goal. With a target number of months, the calculator finds the monthly amount that reaches the goal in that time, allowing for the same monthly interest. Remaining amount = Goal Amount − Already Saved.

Worked example

Inputs: Goal Amount = ₹5,00,000, Already Saved = ₹0, Monthly Savings = ₹10,000, Target Months = (left blank), Annual Return = 4%. Step 1: remaining amount = 5,00,000 − 0 = ₹5,00,000. Step 2: monthly rate = 4 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.003333. Step 3: growing the balance by ₹10,000 a month with interest, it reaches ₹5,00,000 after 47 months. Step 4: that is 3 years and 11 months, with ₹4,70,000 paid in by you and the rest earned as interest.

How to use

  1. Step 1: Enter the total amount you want to save in the "Goal Amount (₹)" field.
  2. Step 2: Enter what you have already saved in the "Already Saved (₹)" field.
  3. Step 3: To find the time needed, enter your monthly saving in the "Monthly Savings (₹)" field and leave "Target Months" blank.
  4. Step 4: To find the monthly amount needed, enter the number of months in the "Target Months" field and leave "Monthly Savings" blank.
  5. Step 5: Enter the interest your savings earn in the "Annual Return % (savings)" field — 4% is the default — then tap Calculate.

Tips

  • Fill in either the monthly saving or the target months, not both — the calculator needs one of them to work out the other.
  • Already-saved money shortens the time a lot, because it starts earning interest from day one.
  • A small increase in the monthly saving cuts the time more than you might expect, because the extra money also earns interest.
  • Keep the goal in a separate savings account so everyday spending does not eat into it.

Common mistakes

  • Filling in both Monthly Savings and Target Months — the calculator will ask you to fill only one.
  • Forgetting the money already saved, which makes the time or monthly amount look larger than it needs to be.
  • Using an unrealistic return rate, such as 12% for a savings account, which makes the plan look easier than it is.
  • Setting the goal amount without checking what the thing actually costs, including taxes and fees.

Frequently asked questions

How long will it take to save ₹5,00,000 at ₹10,000 a month?

At a 4% annual return and starting from zero, it takes 47 months — 3 years and 11 months. You pay in ₹4,70,000 and the rest comes from interest.

How much must I save each month to reach ₹5,00,000 in 24 months?

At a 4% annual return and starting from zero, you need to save about ₹20,046 per month. You would pay in about ₹4,81,099 and earn the rest as interest.

Should I fill in Monthly Savings or Target Months?

Fill in only one. If you know what you can save each month, enter that to find how long the goal takes. If you have a deadline, enter the months to find how much you must save each month.
